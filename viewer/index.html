grazer (brighter = more energy, fainter = more camouflage or deeper in cover) canopy (forest: the only ground that hides a grazer from hunters - and almost nothing grows under it) perception (how far this animal can actually see - free out to 6 tiles, then one spoke on the ring for every energy/tick it burns keeping the extra horizon) flight distance (the ground a grazer keeps between itself and one particular hunter: a few ticks of warning times that hunter's speed, so it is tight around a slow predator and enormous around a fast one - a ghost-faint ring means nothing is in sight and this is what it would keep) too hungry to run (the amber dashed ring, and it is a ring that is collapsing: a grazer may no longer demand more clearance from a predator than half the life it has left. A hunger clause has been in the rules since tick 1728 and never once fired, because it multiplied a gene every grazer here carries at 0.0 - so the starving animal held exactly the same line as the fat one and died holding it, which is what nearly every carcass on this grid actually is. A fed grazer's ring is unchanged to the last pixel; as one burns down its flight ring shrinks in around its own body until it is standing on the plant with a hunter in plain sight. It is the predators' own method turned back on them - they clear ground rather than catch anything, and the hungrier the herd they make, the less of that ground the herd honours - and it is cheap to grant, because a grazer caught standing its ground is worth 8 to the hunter, the same as its corpse.) herd bond (linked grazers are harder for a hunter to single out - brighter = deeper cover) gregariousness (how strongly a grazer walks to company instead of food) breeding bar (the rose collar right on the body: a newborn is now sent out with four ticks of the body it inherits, and the parent has to save up for that first - so a WIDE collar is an expensive animal with a high bar to clear, and a FULL one is an animal that has cleared it) provisioning (the rose core, on either species: how much of itself this animal pours into each newborn on top of that runway - no energy is created, it only moves down a generation) quarry (the orange rim on a grazer: what a hunter would actually bank for catching it - the body plus everything that grazer has managed to eat, minus what is lost crossing between species - so a fed grazer glows to a predator and a starving one is barely worth the chase) hunter (brighter = more energy) digesting (the pale ring: this hunter just ate and cannot hunt again for a few ticks - it still pays full upkeep and still moves, it simply isn't looking for its next meal. Shrinks in as the cooldown burns off. Without this a fat herd was worth eating exactly as fast as a hunter could walk between bodies, which is how 9 grazers and 5 hunters at tick 5984 were both gone by 6050.) acuity (rings around a hunter - sees through camouflage, herds and canopy, costs energy every tick) menace (the wide sparse ring: how much ground this hunter's speed alone clears of feeding grazers, whether or not it ever catches one) nightfall (the cold indigo ring, on both species, appearing only once the light goes: what this animal can actually FIND in the present hour. The day/night wash is no longer decoration - darkness takes up to 1.5 tiles off a hunter's search for live prey, exactly the way the canopy does, and off a grazer's search for a plant, so the safest hours to be prey are also the hungriest ones. A grazer still sees an approaching hunter perfectly well at midnight, a carcass still takes no special eye to find, and the plants nobody finds in the dark stand and ripen until morning. Acuity is a predator's night vision: it is the only thing that buys the dark back.) resource (grows where light reaches the ground, so it clusters in the open - and fattens the longer nobody eats it: small and dark is freshly sprouted and worth what a resource here has always been worth, big and pale is a plant that has stood a hundred ticks, ringed once it can grow no further) grove (the soft amber haze: ground a standing plant can actually sow. A resource left alone for ten ticks sows the next one within three tiles of itself, so food grows in patches instead of an even sprinkle over sixteen hundred tiles - and a population eating every plant the tick it sprouts never lets one reach seed at all, and lives on the flat scatter this world always had. Overlapping haze is a grove, dense and ripe enough for a newborn to feed on the tile it was born on; a bare diamond with no haze is a plant that will be eaten before it ever reproduces.) worth the walk (the dashed amber thread running from a grazer to the ringed plant it has actually chosen. Food used to be taken by distance alone, so the ripening larder and the groves growing out of it were invisible to the only thing in this world that eats them; a grazer now takes the plant worth the most once the walk is paid for - its value on arrival, minus the upkeep of the body going to get it. A short dim thread to the nearest diamond is a flat world of identical sprouts where the choice means nothing and the behaviour is exactly the old one; a long bright thread reaching past close food into a patch of fat pale plants is an animal crossing its whole horizon for a meal worth three of the ones at its feet - and an expensive body, burning more per tick, writes off that distance far harder than a cheap one does.) carcass (a grazer's body - the only carrion a hunter can eat; fades as it rots, and as of this run its SIZE and its orange quarry rim are what a scavenger would actually bank for it. A carcass used to be a flat 30 whatever condition the animal fell in, so a grazer the hunters had starved was worth four times more to them dead than alive; a body is worth the body now, which makes a starveling's corpse a bare speck and an old animal that died fat a genuine windfall.) hunter remains (crossed out: predators no longer live off their own dead, so this is scenery) migrant (just arrived, carries remembered genes) the door (pulsing violet, with the absent species' own shape hollow inside it: where the next migrant of a species that has died out here would choose to come in - a grazer wants ground outside every hunter's menace radius and near something to eat, a hunter wants ground about a sense_range from the nearest prey. A real migrant only glances at two dozen tiles, so it lands near here rather than exactly here.) the latch (a grey bar across a door that is shut: neither species is called in by the calendar any more. A hunter comes only when the grazers standing here are worth the journey - and no single animal, however fat, can open that one. A grazer comes only when enough of the map lies outside every hunter's menace radius to be worth walking into, so a grid covered edge to edge by predators posts nothing more into itself and the besiegers, who cannot eat their own dead, starve the latch back open. Either gate widens, brightens and starts pulsing again as its own condition improves.)
